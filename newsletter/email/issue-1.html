What happened

Your first trip to Silicon Valley

On October 29 we hosted an open evening at Startup Nation Central with Nicole Levin (Pebblebed) and Amit Yampolsky (Rizz), hosted by Avishag Bohbot. The question: how do you make your first trip to the Valley count when it's too early to raise and too late to be a tourist? A few takeaways:

  • Just show up. San Francisco is small and dense, and most of what matters is within a 30-minute walk. Nicole joined a company as employee number seven not because she had something to offer, but because she came.
  • Relationships open slowly. "Introduce me to X" in a first meeting rarely works. Give it a month or three, and come to give before you ask.
  • Be specific. One problem, one type of customer, one question. Book meetings a week or two ahead, and end every meeting by asking who else you should talk to.
  • Tell the story in their language. "I was in 8200" doesn't explain itself. Say what you built, what problem you solved, and why it matters now.
  • How long to stay? No consensus. Amit argued that without a few months on the ground you stay an outsider. Avishag noted that not every founder can leave for half a year, and that two to three weeks with a clear list and clear goals is a strong start. Much of the prep can happen from Israel, including being active on X before you fly.

And as Amit put it: it's not for everyone. There are many ways to build a company, and building from here with a smart bridge to there is a success story too.
